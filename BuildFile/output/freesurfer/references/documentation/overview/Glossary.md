## anatomically derived defect

<span id="line-6" class="anchor"></span>

- A topological defect in the cortical surface that arises from a
  feature of normal neuroanatomy to be distinguished from defects
  arising entirely from segmentation errors. See topological defect
  <span id="line-7" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-8"
  class="anchor"></span>

## artifact

<span id="line-9" class="anchor"></span>

- A feature that appears in an image but is not actually present in the
  imaged object. <span id="line-10"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-11" class="anchor"></span>

## average convexity

<span id="line-12" class="anchor"></span>

- The signed distance that a vertex moves during the inflation process.
  <span id="line-13" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-14"
  class="anchor"></span>

## brain volume

<span id="line-15" class="anchor"></span>

- The T1 volume after the skull and other non-brain structures have been
  removed. This volume can be viewed using tkmedit. <span id="line-16"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-17" class="anchor"></span>

## canonical surface

<span id="line-18" class="anchor"></span>

- Surface-based atlas constructed from the cortical surfaces of 40
  normal individuals (used for inter-subject averaging).
  <span id="line-19" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-20"
  class="anchor"></span>

## conversion/averaging

<span id="line-21" class="anchor"></span>

- Process of converting and averaging multiple structural acquisitions
  from the native magnet format into the native
  [FreeSurfer](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/FreeSurfer)
  format (see COR files). <span id="line-22"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-23" class="anchor"></span>

## COR files

<span id="line-24" class="anchor"></span>

- The native file format used by
  [FreeSurfer](https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/FreeSurfer) to
  store 3D structural image data. <span id="line-25"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-26" class="anchor"></span>

## Euler number

<span id="line-27" class="anchor"></span>

- After Leonhard Euler (1707-83). A topological invariant of a surface
  that can be computed from the number of edges, vertices and faces in a
  polygonal tessellation (command
  <a href="http://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/fswiki/mris_euler_number"
  class="http">mris_euler_number</a> ). The Euler number of a sphere
  will equal 2; the Euler number of a surface with n handles is 2 – 2n.
  <span id="line-28" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-29"
  class="anchor"></span>

## filled volume

<span id="line-30" class="anchor"></span>

- The wm volume after separation of the left and right hemispheres and
  filling of each hemisphere. This volume can be viewed using tkmedit.
  <span id="line-31" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-32"
  class="anchor"></span>

## flattening

<span id="line-33" class="anchor"></span>

- Producing a planar (flat) representation of a patch of the cortical
  surface that has minimal metric distortion. <span id="line-34"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-35" class="anchor"></span>

## gyrus

<span id="line-36" class="anchor"></span>

- A fold or convolution of brain tissue (an outward folded region).
  <span id="line-37" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-38"
  class="anchor"></span>

## hypointensities (wm)

<span id="line-39" class="anchor"></span>

- Dark white matter on a T1-weighted image. <span id="line-40"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-41" class="anchor"></span>

## hypointensities (non-wm)

<span id="line-42" class="anchor"></span>

- Dark gray matter on a T1-weighted image. <span id="line-43"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-44" class="anchor"></span>

## inflated surface

<span id="line-45" class="anchor"></span>

- The smoothwm surface after inflation. This surface can be viewed using
  surfer. <span id="line-46" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-47"
  class="anchor"></span>

## inflation

<span id="line-48" class="anchor"></span>

- The process of smoothing the cortex while minimizing metric
  distortion, so that all sulci are fully visible and surface distances
  are apparent to visual inspection. <span id="line-49"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-50" class="anchor"></span>

## intensity

<span id="line-51" class="anchor"></span>

- Measured amount of magnetic field at a given spatial location,
  represented by a voxel (higher SNR signal to noise ratio means voxels
  will have a higher intensity relative to the background noise, and
  appear brighter). <span id="line-52"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-53" class="anchor"></span>

## label

<span id="line-54" class="anchor"></span>

- A particular region of interest. e.g. in tksurfer the label would be a
  region of interest in the surface. In tkmedit a label is a region of
  interest in the volume. <span id="line-55"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-56" class="anchor"></span>

## morphing

<span id="line-57" class="anchor"></span>

- Computer graphics technique whereby a mapping is computed that
  smoothly transforms one image or surface into another.
  <span id="line-58" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-59"
  class="anchor"></span>

## morphometrics

<span id="line-60" class="anchor"></span>

- The study of geometric properties of the human brain.
  <span id="line-61" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-62"
  class="anchor"></span>

## motion correction

<span id="line-63" class="anchor"></span>

- Processing multiple structural volumes so that the effects of subject
  movement are minimized. This is typically done by aligning multiple
  images/volume to an initial image/volume (see conversion/averaging).
  <span id="line-64" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-65"
  class="anchor"></span>

## MRI volume

<span id="line-66" class="anchor"></span>

- The three dimensional volumetric data set collected from a MRI
  scanner. <span id="line-67" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-68"
  class="anchor"></span>

## orig volume

<span id="line-69" class="anchor"></span>

- The original MRI volume. This volume can be viewed using tkmedit.
  <span id="line-70" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-71"
  class="anchor"></span>

## orig surface

<span id="line-72" class="anchor"></span>

- The first surface constructed by covering the labeled voxels in the
  filled volume. This surface can be viewed using surfer.
  <span id="line-73" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-74"
  class="anchor"></span>

## pial

<span id="line-75" class="anchor"></span>

- Pertaining to the delicate pia mater which envelops the brain (gray
  matter). Also, the model of the pial surface (?h.pial).
  <span id="line-76" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-77"
  class="anchor"></span>

## pial surface

<span id="line-78" class="anchor"></span>

- The refined estimate of the gray/CSF boundary (pial surface). This
  surface can be viewed using surfer. <span id="line-79"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-80" class="anchor"></span>

## region growing process

<span id="line-81" class="anchor"></span>

- An algorithm that groups voxels or sub-regions into larger regions.
  <span id="line-82" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-83"
  class="anchor"></span>

## RF-field inhomogeneities

<span id="line-84" class="anchor"></span>

- Spatial variations in the Radio Frequency (RF) excitation pulse. These
  variations result in changes in the measured intensity for a given
  tissue class that are related to the spatial location of the voxel.
  <span id="line-85" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-86"
  class="anchor"></span>

## segmentation

<span id="line-87" class="anchor"></span>

- Labeling of tissue classes from MRI data (e.g. white matter).
  <span id="line-88" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-89"
  class="anchor"></span>

## smoothing

<span id="line-90" class="anchor"></span>

- Process of producing a relatively even and regular cortical surface.
  <span id="line-91" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-92"
  class="anchor"></span>

## smoothwm surface

<span id="line-93" class="anchor"></span>

- The orig surface after smoothing. This surface can be viewed using
  surfer. <span id="line-94" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-95"
  class="anchor"></span>

## sulcus

<span id="line-96" class="anchor"></span>

- A groove or furrow in brain tissue (an inward folded region).
  <span id="line-97" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-98"
  class="anchor"></span>

## supertessellated icosahedron

<span id="line-99" class="anchor"></span>

- Polygonal approximation to a sphere. <span id="line-100"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-101" class="anchor"></span>

## T1

<span id="line-102" class="anchor"></span>

- Longitudinal relaxation constant. <span id="line-103"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-104" class="anchor"></span>

## T1 volume

<span id="line-105" class="anchor"></span>

- The MRI volume after intensity normalization. This volume can be
  viewed using tkmedit. <span id="line-106"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-107" class="anchor"></span>

## T1 Weighted Image

<span id="line-108" class="anchor"></span>

- A magnetic resonance image where the contrast is predominantly
  dependent on T1. <span id="line-109"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-110" class="anchor"></span>

## T2 Weighted Image

<span id="line-111" class="anchor"></span>

- A magnetic resonance image where the contrast is predominantly
  dependent on T2. <span id="line-112"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-113" class="anchor"></span>

## T2

<span id="line-114" class="anchor"></span>

- Transverse relaxation constant. <span id="line-115"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-116" class="anchor"></span>

## Talairach coordinate

<span id="line-117" class="anchor"></span>

- The corresponding location in the Talairach atlas for a given point in
  a brain that has been coregistered with the atlas (Talairach et al,
  1967). <span id="line-118" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-119"
  class="anchor"></span>

## tessellation

<span id="line-120" class="anchor"></span>

- Covering of a surface by repeated use of a single shape.
  <span id="line-121" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-122"
  class="anchor"></span>

## topology

<span id="line-123" class="anchor"></span>

- The properties of a surface related to its connectivity that are
  unaffected by geometric (i.e. rubber sheet) transformations.
  <span id="line-124" class="anchor"></span><span id="line-125"
  class="anchor"></span>

## topological defect

<span id="line-126" class="anchor"></span>

- A portion of a surface that results in the surface topology differing
  from that of a sphere. <span id="line-127"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-128" class="anchor"></span>

## volume

<span id="line-129" class="anchor"></span>

- A 3-D data set that typically contains either intensity information
  derived from the original MRI, or the results of segmenting this data
  into tissue classes. <span id="line-130"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-131" class="anchor"></span>

## voxel

<span id="line-132" class="anchor"></span>

- The basic element of an MRI volume (analogous to a pixel in a 2-D
  image). The volume of a structural voxel is approximately 1
  mm<sup>3</sup>. <span id="line-133"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-134" class="anchor"></span>

## white surface

<span id="line-135" class="anchor"></span>

- The refined estimate of the gray/white boundary. This surface can be
  viewed using surfer. <span id="line-136"
  class="anchor"></span><span id="line-137" class="anchor"></span>

## wm volume

<span id="line-138" class="anchor"></span>

- The brain volume after white matter segmentation. This is also the
  volume that is manually edited. This volume can be viewed using
  tkmedit. <span id="line-139" class="anchor"></span>
